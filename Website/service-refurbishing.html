01 · Strip and hardware

Strip the Cage and Replace the Hardware

The old screen comes off so the frame can be sanded and the hardware replaced before any paint goes on.

Plastic covers the pool first. Screws and debris stay out of the water while the old screen, fasteners, door, and cables come off.

The aluminum that is still sound stays. This is the work that lets an older cage take a new finish and new screen.

  • Old Screen Removed The screen comes off the walls and the roof so the aluminum is open for sanding and new hardware.
  • 304 Marine-Grade Screws Rusted screws are replaced with 304 marine-grade stainless steel.
  • Sanded Aluminum Oxidation and rough spots are sanded off so the paint has a clean surface to hold.
  • New Doors The enclosure doors are replaced with new doors.
  • New Hurricane Cables Worn hurricane cables come out and new cables go in. They are tightened to finish after the screen is on.
  • New Anchor Screws Anchor screws are replaced along with the rest of the rusted fasteners.
  • Pool Covered Plastic goes over the pool before hardware work starts, so screws and dirt stay out of the water.

02 · Paint

Mask the House and Paint the Aluminum

The house is papered and masked well away from the aluminum. Then the enclosure is painted from the roof down.

The paint is water-based Sherwin-Williams. We mix the color from a sample of the factory aluminum finish, so the painted frame matches the original color as closely as possible.

Two coats. Both are sprayed immediately, from the roof down, in left-to-right passes at a 45-degree angle.

Pool Cage Restoration Questions

What is included in a pool cage restoration?

Three steps on the same enclosure. The old screen comes off, rusted screws are replaced with 304 marine-grade stainless steel, the aluminum is sanded, and the door, hurricane cables, and anchor screws are replaced. The pool is covered during that work. The house is then masked and the frame is painted with two coats of water-based Sherwin-Williams. New screen goes on last, followed by paint touch-ups, door-hardware adjustment, and the final tension on the cables.

How is restoration different from rescreening?

Rescreening replaces the mesh. Restoration also replaces the hardware and paints the aluminum before the new screen goes on. When the frame and hardware can stay, screen repair and rescreening is quoted on its own.

How long does a restoration take?

An average enclosure is usually finished in about 3 to 4 days. Weather and the size of the cage can change the schedule. The timeline is confirmed with the estimate.

What paint do you use?

Water-based Sherwin-Williams. Two coats, sprayed immediately from the top of the enclosure down, in left-to-right passes at a 45-degree angle. See how we paint.

Can you match the original aluminum color?

Yes. The color is mixed from a sample of the factory aluminum finish, so the painted frame matches the original color as closely as possible.

What hardware gets replaced?

Rusted screws are replaced with 304 marine-grade stainless steel. Anchor screws, the enclosure door, and the hurricane cables are replaced in the first step. The cables are tightened to finish after the new screen is on, and the door hardware is adjusted before we leave.

Do you protect the pool and the house?

Yes. Plastic covers the pool during the hardware work so screws and debris stay out of the water. Before painting, the house is papered and masked well away from the aluminum so overspray stays off the walls, windows, and trim.

Which screen can I choose?

The mesh is your choice: 18/14 standard, 20/20 no-see-um, heavy-duty polyester, PetScreen, Florida Glass, or shade screen. See the screens we install.

Can I add a Clearview during the restoration?

Yes. While the cage is open, the standard post design can change to a Clearview on one section or on every wall. The screen is polyester 16/14, or 17/20 no-see-um polyester. The enclosure keeps its strength and its supports.

What warranty is included?

A 3-year warranty covers our labor. Screen materials carry the manufacturer's warranty.

Is a restoration less expensive than a new enclosure?

When the frame is still sound, restoration costs a fraction of a new build and an average cage is usually finished in about 3 to 4 days. The price is set with a free estimate.

Do I need a permit?

Permits are not required for refurbishing and painting. If a project goes beyond a restoration, we tell you what applies before work starts.

Which areas do you serve?

Sarasota, Manatee, and Charlotte counties, including Sarasota, Bradenton, Venice, North Port, Port Charlotte, and Lakewood Ranch. See the full list on Areas We Serve.
