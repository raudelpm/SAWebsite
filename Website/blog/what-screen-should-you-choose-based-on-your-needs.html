What Screen Should You Choose Based on Your Needs?

Updated Apr 14, 2026 • 18/14 vs 20x20 screen options

Choosing the right screen for your pool cage or lanai isn’t just about price—it’s about comfort, location, and how you use your space. Two of the most common options are 18/14 standard screen and 20x20 no-see-um screen.

Each one is better depending on your situation.

First: what actually changes between screens?

The main difference is the mesh size (how tight the screen is):

  • 18/14 → larger openings
  • 20x20 → smaller openings

Smaller openings = more protection, less airflow.
Larger openings = more airflow, less protection.

The tighter 20x20 mesh is designed to block tiny insects like no-see-ums, while 18/14 focuses more on airflow and visibility. For a deeper comparison, see our post on 20x20 no-see-um vs 18/14 screen.

Choose 18/14 screen if you want comfort & airflow

Best for:

  • Hot areas where airflow matters
  • Pool cages with good breeze
  • Homes without heavy insect problems

Why choose it:

  • Better ventilation
  • Clearer view
  • More affordable
  • Easier to clean

This is the most common screen used in Florida pool cages because it balances performance and comfort.

Choose 20x20 no-see-um screen if bugs are the problem

Best for:

  • Homes near water, lakes, or woods
  • Areas with no-see-ums or biting insects
  • People sensitive to bug bites

Why choose it:

  • Blocks tiny insects
  • Better protection overall
  • More privacy

The tighter mesh can stop small pests like gnats and sandflies that standard screens cannot block.

Real-life decision guide

  • Go with 18/14 if you want airflow and comfort, bugs aren’t a big issue, and you want the most cost-effective option.
  • Go with 20x20 if you can’t deal with bugs anymore, you live near water/vegetation, or you want a more enclosed feel.

Mix both (smart strategy)

A lot of homeowners don’t know this, but you can mix screens:

  • Install 20x20 on lower panels (where bugs enter)
  • Install 18/14 on upper panels (for airflow)

This gives you the best of both worlds.

Cost difference

20x20 is usually about 10–15% more expensive than 18/14. Over years of use, the difference is often small compared to the comfort.
