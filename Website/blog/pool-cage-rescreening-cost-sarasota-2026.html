How Much Does Pool Cage Rescreening Cost in Sarasota, FL? (2026 Guide)

Updated Apr 24, 2026 • Sarasota pool cage rescreening

Introduction

If your pool cage screen is torn, loose, or simply worn out, one of the first questions that comes to mind is: how much will it cost to fix it? In Sarasota, Bradenton, and surrounding areas, pricing can vary depending on several factors. In this guide, we’ll break it down so you know exactly what to expect.

Average cost of pool cage rescreening in Florida

On average, homeowners in Sarasota County can expect:

  • Single panel repair: $70 – $150
  • Partial rescreen (multiple panels): $200 – $600
  • Full pool cage rescreen: $1,200 – $3,500+

The final price depends on the size of your enclosure, type of screen, and overall condition.

What affects the price?

1. Size of the pool cage

Larger enclosures require more material and labor, which increases the total cost.

2. Type of screen material

Different screens come at different price points:

  • 18/14 Standard Screen – Most affordable option
  • 20/20 No-See-Um Screen – Better insect protection
  • Polyester Screen (No-See-Um upgrade) – Stronger & longer-lasting
  • Pet Screen (14/10) – Extra durability
  • Super Screen – Premium strength & longevity
  • Florida Glass – Privacy screen for bottom panels
  • Sun Shade 40/40 – Reduces heat and glare

3. Condition of the structure

If your frame has damage, loose screws, or corrosion, additional repairs may be needed before installing new screen.

4. Labor & accessibility

Hard-to-reach areas or second-story cages can slightly increase labor costs.

Is it better to repair or replace everything?

If only a few panels are damaged, repairs are usually enough. However, if your screen is faded, brittle, or over 7–10 years old, a full rescreen is often the better investment.

How to save money

  • Fix small tears early before they spread
  • Replace multiple panels at once
  • Choose the right screen for long-term durability

Conclusion

Pool cage rescreening in Sarasota isn’t one-size-fits-all—but knowing the factors helps you make the right decision and avoid overpaying.
