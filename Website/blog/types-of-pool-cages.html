Types of Pool Cages: Which Screen Enclosure Is Best for Your Home?

Updated May 8, 2026 • Pool cage styles in Florida

Pool cages are one of the most popular outdoor features in Florida homes. They help protect your pool area from insects, debris, leaves, and harsh sun exposure while creating a more comfortable outdoor living space.

But not all pool cages are the same.

There are several different types of pool enclosures, each designed for different needs, budgets, and home styles. In this guide, we’ll explain the most common types of pool cages and the benefits of each one.

What is a pool cage?

A pool cage is an aluminum-framed screen enclosure built around a swimming pool, patio, or lanai area. The structure is covered with screening material that allows airflow while helping keep bugs and debris out.

Pool cages are extremely common in Florida because they:

  • Reduce pool maintenance
  • Improve outdoor comfort
  • Provide shade and UV protection
  • Help keep insects away
  • Add value and curb appeal

1. Standard pool cage

The standard pool cage is the most common type of enclosure found in Florida homes.

It features:

  • Aluminum framing
  • Multiple vertical support beams
  • Screen walls and roof panels
  • Basic functional design

Pros

  • Most affordable option
  • Durable and reliable
  • Easy to repair
  • Meets most residential needs

Best for

  • Homeowners looking for a cost-effective and practical enclosure solution

2. Clear View / panoramic pool cage

A Clear View pool cage—also called a panoramic enclosure—removes several vertical aluminum supports to create a more open view.

This design is extremely popular in homes with:

  • Waterfront views
  • Golf course views
  • Scenic backyards

Pros

  • Cleaner, modern appearance
  • Better visibility
  • Fewer visual obstructions
  • Luxury aesthetic

Cons

  • Higher cost
  • Larger screen sections
  • More specialized installation

Best for: homeowners who want a modern and high-end outdoor appearance. Read more in our guide to Clear View conversions.

3. Mansard pool cage

A mansard pool cage has a roof design where the sides angle inward before connecting to a flat center section.

This style creates:

  • A taller appearance
  • Improved rain runoff
  • Extra overhead space

Pros

  • Elegant architectural look
  • Better water drainage
  • Strong structural design

Best for

  • Homes wanting a more decorative or upscale enclosure style

4. Dome pool cage

A dome pool cage features a rounded or curved roof design.

This style is less common but offers:

  • Unique appearance
  • Strong wind resistance
  • Improved rain runoff

Pros

  • Distinctive look
  • Good durability
  • Efficient water shedding

Cons

  • More expensive
  • Specialized construction

Best for: homeowners wanting a custom or architectural enclosure design.

5. Gable roof pool cage

A gable-style enclosure features a peaked roof similar to traditional house roofing.

This design allows:

  • Higher ceilings
  • Better airflow
  • Spacious appearance

Pros

  • Great ventilation
  • Attractive design
  • More open feeling

Best for

  • Larger pool areas and outdoor entertainment spaces

6. Flat roof screen enclosure

Flat roof enclosures are commonly used for:

  • Patios
  • Porches
  • Small pool areas
  • Entryways

They feature a simpler roof design with lower height.

Pros

  • Lower cost
  • Simple installation
  • Great for smaller spaces

Cons

  • Less dramatic appearance
  • Reduced airflow compared to taller structures

Best for: budget-friendly projects and compact outdoor areas.

7. Two-story pool cage

Two-story enclosures are designed for homes with:

  • Multiple floors
  • Balconies
  • Tall patio areas

These structures extend upward to fully enclose larger spaces.

Pros

  • Covers large vertical areas
  • Perfect for multi-level homes
  • Creates massive outdoor living space

Cons

  • Higher material and labor costs
  • More engineering required

Best for: large Florida homes and luxury properties.

8. Custom pool cage designs

Some homeowners choose fully custom enclosures designed specifically for their property.

Custom options may include:

  • Panoramic sections
  • Specialty doors
  • Integrated lighting
  • Privacy screens
  • Decorative framing
  • Oversized layouts

Best for: homeowners wanting a completely personalized outdoor space.

Which pool cage is best?

The best pool cage depends on:

  • Your budget
  • Home design
  • Desired appearance
  • Backyard view
  • Wind exposure
  • Long-term goals
  • If you want affordability: a standard pool cage may be the best option.
  • If you want luxury and visibility: a Clear View panoramic enclosure is often the preferred choice.
  • If you want architectural style: mansard, dome, or gable designs can add a more upscale appearance.

Final thoughts

A pool cage is more than just a screen enclosure—it’s an extension of your outdoor living space.

Choosing the right type of pool cage can improve:

  • Comfort
  • Appearance
  • Property value
  • Outdoor enjoyment
  • Long-term durability

Whether you need a new enclosure, a rescreen, structural repairs, or a full restoration, choosing the right design is one of the most important decisions for your Florida home.
